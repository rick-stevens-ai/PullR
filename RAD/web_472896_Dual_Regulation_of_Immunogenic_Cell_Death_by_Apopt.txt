Title: Dual Regulation of Immunogenic Cell Death by Apoptotic Caspases Following Combined Radiation and ATR Inhibitor Treatment

Abstract:
Inhibitors of the ATR kinase function as radiosensitizers by disrupting the G2 checkpoint and impairing DNA repair mechanisms. Recent evidence indicates that ATR inhibitors may also enhance radiation-induced antitumor immunity, although the precise immunomodulatory mechanisms remain unclear, particularly regarding their relationship with caspase-dependent apoptotic pathways. This study investigates whether ATR inhibition, in combination with irradiation, augments the presentation of hallmark factors of immunogenic cell death (ICD), and examines the regulatory role of caspase activation in this context. Human lung cancer and osteosarcoma cell lines (SW900, H1975, H460, U2OS) were treated with X-rays and ATR inhibitors (VE822; AZD6738), both with and without a pan-caspase inhibitor. ICD hallmarks—HMGB1 release, ATP secretion, and calreticulin surface presentation—were assessed using immunoblotting, the CellTiter-Glo assay, and an optimized live-cell flow cytometry assay, respectively, with normalization to a barcoded control sample for accurate quantification. The combined treatment increased extracellular HMGB1 release in all cell lines at 72 hours, an effect largely—but not exclusively—correlated with loss of plasma membrane integrity and suppressed by caspase inhibition. Notably, one cell line exhibited HMGB1 release despite caspase inhibition, accompanied by induction of pMLKL, a necroptosis marker. ATP secretion was observed as early as 48 hours post-treatment, independent of membrane integrity loss, and was further enhanced by pan-caspase inhibition. Calreticulin surface presentation increased following irradiation but was not further affected by ATR or caspase inhibition. These findings demonstrate that ATR inhibition enhances the presentation of two out of three ICD hallmark factors in irradiated human cancer cells. Furthermore, caspase activation differentially regulates these factors, suggesting a dual role in modulating tumor immunogenicity by promoting both immunostimulatory and immunosuppressive effects.
