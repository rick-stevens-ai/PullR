Title: Multivalent Binding of Tardigrade Dsup Protein to Chromatin Enhances Yeast Survival and Longevity Under Oxidative Stress

Abstract:
Tardigrades are renowned for their extraordinary ability to withstand extreme environmental conditions. The damage suppressor (Dsup) protein has been implicated in their remarkable resistance to reactive oxygen species (ROS) generated by irradiation. In this study, we demonstrate that expression of the Ramazzottius varieornatus Dsup protein in Saccharomyces cerevisiae reduces oxidative DNA damage and extends the lifespan of budding yeast exposed to chronic oxidative genotoxicity. The protective effect against ROS requires either the Dsup HMGN-like domain or sequences located C-terminal to this domain. Dsup associates broadly across the yeast genome, utilizing multiple modes of nucleosome binding: the HMGN-like region interacts with both the H2A/H2B acidic patch and H3/H4 histone tails, while the C-terminal region binds directly to DNA. These findings establish a precedent for engineering organisms to physically shield their genomes, thereby promoting survival and longevity in the presence of oxidative damage.
