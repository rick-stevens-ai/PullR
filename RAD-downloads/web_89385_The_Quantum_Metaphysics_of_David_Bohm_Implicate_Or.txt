Title: The Quantum Metaphysics of David Bohm: Implicate Order, Consciousness, and the Unification of Mind and Matter
Authors: Thomas J. Germine
Year: 1995
Paper ID: web_89385
URL: http://www.goertzel.org/dynapsyc/1995/TGERMINE.html
Venue: DynaPsych
Content Status: LLM_PROCESSED, WEB_SCRAPED, DOCUMENT_CLEANED

Abstract:
This article examines the metaphysical framework developed by physicist David Bohm, with particular emphasis on his concepts of "implicate order" and "soma-significance" as foundational to a new paradigm that seeks to unify mind and matter. Bohm's approach challenges the traditional Mind-Matter Dualism by positing an underlying order of pure information—the implicate order—from which observable phenomena, or the explicate order, unfold. In contrast to the mainstream Copenhagen Interpretation of quantum mechanics, which treats quantum phenomena as epistemological constructs, Bohm asserts a literal ontological reality to these phenomena. The discussion explores the philosophical implications of Bohm's quantum theory, including the role of consciousness in shaping reality and the deep interconnectedness of mind and matter. Drawing on both historical philosophical traditions and modern physics, the article situates Bohm's ideas within a broader context and argues that his quantum metaphysics offers a promising path beyond the dualities that have long characterized Western thought.
